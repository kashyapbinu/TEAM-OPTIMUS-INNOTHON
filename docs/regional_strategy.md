## Justification by region
**North: solar exporter with Himalayan firm hydro.** Rajasthan has the largest solar potential of any state and the largest
150 m wind potential. HP, Uttarakhand and J&K hold about a third (33%) of India's exploitable hydro potential. The load-centre coal fleet in UP,
Punjab and Haryana gets no new units and runs out its life. Nuclear grows at Rawatbhata, Mahi Banswara and Gorakhpur.
Solar reaches its 44%-of-potential ceiling (913 TWh), so the North is solar-led and a modest net exporter.

**West: firm-power backbone in transition.** Pithead coal in Chhattisgarh and MP is the cheapest firm thermal power. It is retained
(it grows to ~810 TWh by 2035 and holds there) as the balancing reserve for the national VRE build. Gujarat has the most 40%+ CUF wind land in India.
Nuclear sites at Tarapur, Jaitapur, Kakrapar, Mithi Virdi and Chutka. The West has the highest absolute emissions, so it is the
priority for coal flexibilisation, efficiency retrofits and CCUS-readiness.

**South: the cleanest large region.** Tamil Nadu, Karnataka and AP have India's best wind after Gujarat, strong solar, and
the Kudankulam–Kalpakkam–Kaiga–Kovvada nuclear cluster. Monsoon wind and dry-season solar complement each other.
93% clean by 2047.

**East: coal heartland that imports clean power.** Its population-driven requirement more than triples (Bihar, WB). Wind potential
is negligible and ground-solar potential is concentrated in Odisha and Jharkhand (NISE 2025). Pithead coal (Jharkhand, Odisha) is capped at
1.6× today, so production shifts −192 TWh to the North, West and South. Ground, rooftop and floating solar (Hirakud) all reach the
44% developable ceiling. The East needs a just-transition plan for
coal districts and is a CCUS pilot candidate.

**North-East: hydro export hub.** Arunachal holds 38% of India's exploitable hydro potential. Development is modelled at
60% of the normal rate (seismic, ecological, downstream concerns). Evacuation through the Siliguri corridor needs HVDC.
Production rises to about 2.2× the region's own BAU requirement. Assam and Tripura gas provides local flexibility.

## Policy hooks
Green Energy Corridors and inter-regional HVDC; Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (carbon-price trajectory); National Green
Hydrogen Mission; Nuclear Energy Mission; PM Surya Ghar rooftop; pumped-storage policy; CEA coal flexibilisation;
Brahmaputra-basin hydro evacuation master plan; net-zero 2070 pathway.
